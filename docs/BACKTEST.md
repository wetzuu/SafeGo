# SafeGo weather backtest

Generated by `npm run backtest`. Do not edit by hand; change `data/backtest/events.json` and rerun.

## Result

- **Flood days rated High or above:** 8 of 8
- **Dry days rated High or above:** 0 of 7

## What this shows

- On 2 of 8 flagged flood days the rain amount alone set the rating. On the others it was the model's weather type (thunderstorm or heavy rain): the model's rain amounts were far below what PAGASA's gauges measured, so the weather type is what carries the warning.
- 7 of 7 dry days still reached Moderate at their worst hour, mostly from afternoon wind gusts or a brief shower. Only the High line separates flood days from dry days.

## Method

- **Days:** documented Metro Manila flood days (dated reports of flooded or impassable roads, or a state of calamity) and dry-season days with heat-index class suspensions. Each day links to its source below.
- **Weather:** Open-Meteo's archive of past forecasts at the 18 SafeGo places, the same models the live app reads. The raw responses are saved in `data/backtest/raw/`.
- **Scoring:** the app's own `RainfallScoring.assessPastDays`, unchanged: the strongest of sky conditions, wind gusts, the wettest hour, and more than 65 mm in three hours (PAGASA's red threshold).
- **Rule:** A day counts as flagged when any SafeGo place scores High (60) or above. That is the worst rating the map would show that day.

## Limits

- **Weather only.** PAGASA alerts are not tested: there is no public archive of past CAP alerts.
- **Model rain, not gauges.** Weather models smooth out the heaviest downpours, so the rain amounts here are usually lower than PAGASA's rain gauges measured on the same day.
- **Small sample.** These are a handful of well-documented days, not a full season. They show whether the scoring reacts to real events; they are not an accuracy rate.
- **Whole-day view.** Each day is scored at its worst hour; the live app scores the current hour.
